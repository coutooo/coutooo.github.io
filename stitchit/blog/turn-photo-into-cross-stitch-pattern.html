How to turn a photo into a cross stitch pattern

A pet, a wedding photo, the view from your favourite holiday. Almost any photo can become a cross stitch pattern. The secret is in a few choices you make before and after the conversion.

By Manuel Couto Updated 15 Nov 2026 6 min read
📸 ➜ 🧵

1. Choose a photo that will stitch well

  • One clear subject. A pet's face works better than a crowded group shot.
  • Good contrast. The subject should stand out from the background.
  • Simple background. Or crop it away. Busy backgrounds turn into confetti.
  • Even light. Harsh shadows become big dark blocks of stitches.

Crop tightly around what matters before converting. Every square you remove is fewer stitches.

2. Pick the size in stitches

The pattern size is measured in stitches, and the finished size depends on your fabric. On 14-count Aida, 14 stitches make one inch (2.54 cm):

Pattern widthOn 14-countOn 18-count
70 stitches5 in / 12.7 cm3.9 in / 9.9 cm
100 stitches7.1 in / 18.1 cm5.6 in / 14.1 cm
150 stitches10.7 in / 27.2 cm8.3 in / 21.2 cm

More stitches means more detail, and more hours. For portraits, around 100 to 150 stitches wide is a good balance. See our Aida count guide for choosing fabric.

3. Limit the number of colours

Photos have millions of colours; patterns need a few dozen at most. Fewer colours means a cleaner look, fewer thread changes and a cheaper shopping list.

  • 10–15 colours: bold, graphic, great for beginners.
  • 20–30 colours: the sweet spot for pets and portraits.
  • 40+ colours: realistic, but slow and full of confetti stitches.

4. Match colours to real DMC threads

A pattern is only useful if you can buy the colours. Converting straight to the DMC palette, the most widely sold embroidery floss, means every colour on the chart is a thread number you can find in a shop.

5. Clean up the chart

Automatic conversion gets you 90% there. The last 10% is what makes it look hand-designed:

  1. Remove single stray stitches of an odd colour ("confetti").
  2. Fix the eyes; a few hand-placed stitches bring a face to life.
  3. Simplify the background into one or two colours.
  4. Merge colours that look almost the same.

Frequently asked questions

Can any photo be made into a cross stitch pattern?

Almost any, but photos with one clear subject, good contrast and a simple background give the best results.

How many colours should a cross stitch pattern have?

Around 20 to 30 DMC colours works well for pets and portraits. Simpler designs look great with 10 to 15.

What size should my pattern be?

For a portrait, 100 to 150 stitches wide is a good balance of detail and time. On 14-count Aida, 100 stitches is about 18 cm wide.
