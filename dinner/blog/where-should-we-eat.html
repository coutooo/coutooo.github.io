"Where should we eat?" 7 ways to finally decide

"I don't mind, you choose." "No, you choose." Sound familiar? Here are seven simple systems couples, friends and big groups use to pick a restaurant in minutes — and actually enjoy it.

By Manuel Couto Updated 26 Oct 2026 6 min read
🍣 🍕 🌮 🍜

Why is choosing a restaurant so hard?

It's not that anyone is being difficult. Picking where to eat stacks up three things that make any decision harder:

  • Too many options. A map app shows dozens of places within walking distance. More choice feels good in theory, but makes it harder to commit.
  • Nobody wants to be blamed. If you pick and it's mediocre, it's "your" bad dinner. Saying "I don't mind" feels safer.
  • It happens at the end of the day. After hours of work decisions, the last thing anyone wants is another one.

The fix isn't trying harder. It's using a simple system that shrinks the options and spreads the responsibility. Here are seven that work.

1. The 5-3-1 rule

The classic, and for good reason:

  1. Person A names five places they'd be happy with.
  2. Person B narrows those down to three.
  3. Person A makes the final pick.

Everyone gets a say, nobody carries the whole decision, and it takes about a minute. For three people, use 5-3-2-1 and pass it around.

2. Veto, don't pick

People are often bad at saying what they want but very good at saying what they don't. Give everyone one or two vetoes. Someone suggests a place; if nobody uses a veto, that's dinner. It turns "I don't know" into a quick yes or no.

3. Set three filters before you look

Before anyone opens a map, agree on just three things:

  • Budget — quick and cheap, or a proper night out?
  • Distance — walking, short drive, or delivery?
  • Must-haves — open now, vegetarian options, kid-friendly, good rating.

Those three answers usually cut the list from fifty places to five. Choosing between five is easy.

4. Take turns being "the picker"

Alternate who decides — this week it's you, next time it's them. The picker chooses, and the rule is no complaining. It works especially well for couples, and you'll each discover places the other would never have tried.

5. Keep a rotation list

Make a shared note of ten go-to spots you both like, grouped by mood: comfort food, healthy, celebration, cheap and quick. On a tired weeknight, pick a category and take the next place on the list. Add any new favourites as you find them.

6. Let chance decide — and watch your reaction

Flip a coin or spin a wheel between two finalists. The trick isn't the coin: it's how you feel when it lands. If you're secretly disappointed, you've just found out what you really wanted. Go with that.

7. Swipe together

This is the one that ends the debate for good. Everyone gets the same shortlist of nearby restaurants and swipes on their own phone — right for "yes", left for "no". Nobody sees anyone else's choices, so there's no pressure and no polite "I don't mind". The first place everyone likes wins.

Extra tips for big groups

  • Ask about dietary needs first. Allergies, vegetarian or halal — knowing early saves an awkward change of plan.
  • Pick the area before the restaurant. It's much easier to agree on a neighbourhood than on a place.
  • Vote privately. Public votes drift towards whatever the loudest person likes.
  • Set a deadline. "We decide by 7pm" stops the group chat dragging on.
  • Book ahead. Once you've chosen, reserve a table — large groups often can't just walk in.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 5-3-1 rule for choosing a restaurant?

One person names five restaurants, the second narrows them to three, and the first makes the final pick. Everyone has a say and nobody is stuck with the whole decision.

How can a big group decide where to eat quickly?

Agree on the hard limits first (area, budget, dietary needs, open now), then let everyone vote privately on a short list — for example by swiping in a shared session — and go with the place most people liked.

How do couples stop arguing about where to eat?

Use a system instead of negotiating every time: take turns being the picker, give each person a veto, or both swipe through nearby places and go with the first match.
