Skin types

What's my skin type? The Fitzpatrick scale explained

Two people can spend the same hour in the sun and come home very differently. One is fine, the other is red. Your skin type is the reason, and knowing it helps you plan time outdoors.

By Manuel Couto Updated 15 Nov 2026 5 min read
🎨 ☀️ 🧑

What the Fitzpatrick scale is

The Fitzpatrick scale was developed by dermatologist Thomas Fitzpatrick in 1975 to describe how different skin reacts to UV light. It's still used by dermatologists today. It groups skin into six types based on how easily it burns and how easily it tans.

The six skin types

TypeTypical featuresSun reaction
IVery fair, often red or blond hair, frecklesAlways burns, never tans
IIFair, light eyesBurns easily, tans minimally
IIILight to mediumSometimes burns, tans gradually
IVOlive or light brownRarely burns, tans easily
VBrownVery rarely burns, tans very easily
VIDark brown to blackAlmost never burns

How to find your type in a minute

Ask yourself two questions about the first strong sun of the year, without sunscreen:

  1. Do I burn? Always, usually, sometimes, rarely or never.
  2. Do I tan afterwards? Never, a little, gradually, easily or deeply.

Match your answers to the table. If you're between two types, assume the fairer one; it's the safer choice. A dermatologist can confirm your type during a skin check.

Why your skin type matters

  • Types I and II burn fastest and have the highest risk of sun damage. Take precautions from a UV index of 3 upward, and keep sun exposure short.
  • Types III and IV burn less often but still build up damage. Sunscreen and shade still matter.
  • Types V and VI rarely burn, but UV still damages skin and eyes, and skin cancer can still occur, so protection and regular skin checks are still important.
🩺

Check your skin. Whatever your type, see a doctor about any new mole or a spot that changes in size, shape or colour.

Frequently asked questions

How many skin types are there?

The Fitzpatrick scale has six skin types, from type I (always burns, never tans) to type VI (almost never burns).

Can dark skin get sunburnt?

Yes, though less often. All skin types can be damaged by UV, so sun protection matters for everyone.

Does my skin type change?

No. A tan temporarily darkens your skin, but your Fitzpatrick type is based on your natural, unexposed skin and how it reacts to the sun.
