Eating out with a picky eater: how to pick a place everyone likes

One friend eats "everything except vegetables, fish, spice and anything new". Here's how to find a restaurant that keeps them happy without making everyone else eat plain pasta.

By Manuel Couto Updated 15 Nov 2026 5 min read
🥦 🙅 🍕

Restaurants that work for almost everyone

  • Build-your-own: poke, burritos, Korean BBQ, hot pot. Everyone picks their own ingredients.
  • Sharing plates: tapas, mezze, dim sum. The picky eater eats what they like; nobody notices.
  • Big menus with a "simple" section: Italian, diners, gastropubs.
  • Food halls: one table, ten kitchens.

What to check before you go

  1. Ask about real restrictions vs preferences. An allergy is non-negotiable; "not a fan of mushrooms" isn't.
  2. Read the menu online and make sure there are at least two dishes they'd happily order.
  3. Check if dishes can be adjusted. Many places will hold the sauce or swap a side.
  4. For allergies, call ahead. Ask how they handle cross-contamination.

At the table

Don't make it a topic. Comments like "you should try this" turn dinner into a negotiation. Let everyone order what they want, and suggest sharing a few plates so the adventurous eaters still get to try new things.

Frequently asked questions

What type of restaurant is best for picky eaters?

Build-your-own places, sharing-plate restaurants and food halls work well because everyone can choose exactly what they eat.

How do I check if a restaurant suits dietary restrictions?

Read the menu online, check for clearly labelled dishes, and call ahead for allergies to ask how they handle cross-contamination.

How do we choose a restaurant everyone agrees on?

Let everyone vote on a shortlist. Swipe apps like Dinner show only the places that everyone in the group liked.
