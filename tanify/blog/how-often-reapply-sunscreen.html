Sunscreen

How often should you reapply sunscreen? (And how much to use)

Most sunburns happen to people who did put sunscreen on. They just used too little, or forgot to reapply. Here's how to get it right.

By Manuel Couto Updated 15 Nov 2026 5 min read
🧴 ⏱️ 🏖️

The short answer: every 2 hours

Dermatologists generally recommend reapplying sunscreen at least every two hours when you're outdoors, and right after swimming, sweating heavily or towelling off, even if the bottle says "water resistant". Water-resistant sunscreens are tested to keep working for 40 or 80 minutes in water, not all day.

How much sunscreen is enough?

Most people apply a quarter to half of what's needed, which means far less protection than the SPF on the label.

  • Body: about a shot glass (roughly 30 ml) for an adult in swimwear.
  • Face and neck: about half a teaspoon, or two finger-lengths of lotion.
  • Don't forget: ears, back of the neck, tops of the feet, scalp partings and lips (use an SPF lip balm).

Apply about 15 minutes before you go out so it can form an even layer.

What SPF actually means

SPF measures protection against UVB, the rays that cause burning. Under test conditions, SPF 30 blocks about 97% of UVB and SPF 50 about 98%. No sunscreen blocks 100%, and a higher number doesn't mean you can stay out longer without reapplying.

Look for broad spectrum (or a UVA logo) too, which means it also protects against UVA, the rays linked to skin ageing. For time outdoors, most health agencies recommend broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher.

6 mistakes that still leave you burnt

  1. Using too little. Half the amount gives you far less than half the protection.
  2. Applying once in the morning. It wears off with sweat, water, towels and time.
  3. Relying on make-up SPF. You'd need to apply far more foundation than anyone uses.
  4. Skipping cloudy days. UV gets through clouds.
  5. Using last summer's bottle. Check the expiry date; heat breaks sunscreen down.
  6. Treating sunscreen as a licence to stay out. It works best together with shade, a hat and avoiding the midday peak.

Never forget to reapply

The hardest part is remembering. Tanify sends SPF reapply alerts during your session, alongside the live UV index and burn-risk warnings, so you don't lose track of time on the beach.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to reapply sunscreen if I'm not swimming?

Yes. Sunscreen wears off through sweat, rubbing and time, so reapply at least every two hours while you're outdoors.

Is SPF 50 twice as strong as SPF 30?

No. SPF 30 blocks about 97% of UVB and SPF 50 about 98%. Applying enough and reapplying matters more than the number.

Should I wear sunscreen on cloudy days?

Yes, when the UV index is 3 or higher. Clouds block much less UV than they appear to.
