How to put in and take out contact lenses: a beginner's guide

The first few times feel impossible. Then, a week later, it takes ten seconds. Here's the step-by-step routine that gets you there faster.

By Manuel Couto Updated 15 Nov 2026 6 min read
👆 👁️ ✨

Before you start

  • Wash and dry your hands with a lint-free towel. Clean hands are the most important step.
  • Keep nails short so you don't scratch the lens or your eye.
  • Work over a clean table, not an open sink.
  • Always start with the same eye so you never mix up left and right lenses.

Check the lens isn't inside out

Put the lens on the tip of your index finger and look at it from the side. A correct lens looks like a bowl with edges curving up. If the edges flare outwards like a saucer, it's inside out. Some lenses also have tiny numbers on the edge that read correctly when the lens is the right way round.

How to put in a contact lens

  1. Place the lens on the tip of the index finger of your dominant hand.
  2. With your other hand, hold your upper lid up at the lash line.
  3. Use the middle finger of your lens hand to pull the lower lid down.
  4. Look up or straight ahead and gently place the lens on your eye.
  5. Release the lids slowly, look down, then blink a few times to settle it.

How to take out a contact lens

  1. Wash and dry your hands.
  2. Look up and pull your lower lid down with your middle finger.
  3. With your index finger, slide the lens down onto the white of your eye.
  4. Gently pinch it off with your thumb and index finger.
  5. Throw away daily lenses, or clean and store reusable ones in fresh solution.
💡

Nervous? Practise touching the white of your eye with a clean, wet fingertip. Your eye gets used to it in a few days. If a lens feels uncomfortable after inserting, take it out, rinse it with solution and try again.

Never do this

  • Rinse lenses or the case with tap water.
  • Wet a lens with saliva.
  • Top up old solution instead of replacing it.
  • Keep wearing a lens that hurts.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my contact lens is inside out?

Hold it on your fingertip and look from the side. A correct lens forms a bowl with edges curving up; an inside-out lens has edges flaring out like a saucer.

How long does it take to get used to putting in contacts?

Most people get comfortable within one to two weeks of daily practice.

What if I can't get my contact lens out?

Stay calm, wash your hands, add a few drops of lens-compatible rewetting drops and try again. If you can't remove it or your eye hurts, contact your eye care professional.
