Date night

Date night restaurant ideas: how to pick the perfect spot

The right restaurant can make a date — the wrong one can make it hard to even hear each other. Here are ideas for every kind of date night, and a quick checklist for choosing the place.

By Manuel Couto Updated 26 Oct 2026 6 min read
🕯️ 🍷 🍝

The 5-point vibe checklist

Before you book, run the place through these five questions:

  1. Can you hear each other? Check photos and reviews for words like "loud" or "buzzing".
  2. Is the seating good for two? Small tables, booths or a counter beat a long communal bench.
  3. Does the menu suit you both? Check for dietary needs and dislikes before you go.
  4. Is the price comfortable? A date shouldn't feel like a financial stress test.
  5. Is it easy to get to — and to go on from? A bar or a walk nearby gives you a natural next step.

First dates

Keep it relaxed, mid-priced and easy to leave or extend.

  • Tapas or small-plates bar — order a few dishes; stay for more if it's going well.
  • Wine bar with snacks — less formal than a full dinner.
  • A good pizza place — low pressure, everyone likes pizza.
  • Food market — you each pick something and share; easy conversation starter.

Anniversaries & special occasions

  • Tasting menu — let the kitchen take care of the evening.
  • Omakase or chef's counter — watching the chef is part of the experience.
  • Rooftop or a table with a view — book for sunset.
  • The place where you had your first date — a little nostalgia goes a long way.
💡

Tip: mention the occasion when you book. Many restaurants will give you a nicer table or a small surprise.

Easy weeknight dates

  • Your favourite local — a regular spot is underrated.
  • Ramen or pho — cosy, quick and affordable.
  • A new neighbourhood place you keep walking past.
  • Brunch instead of dinner — at the weekend, when you're both less tired.

Something a bit different

  • Korean BBQ or hot pot — cooking together at the table breaks the ice.
  • A cuisine neither of you has tried — Ethiopian, Georgian, Peruvian.
  • A progressive dinner — starter in one place, main in another, dessert in a third.
  • Supper club or pop-up — check local listings.

Deciding together (without the back-and-forth)

Planning a surprise? Use the checklist and book. Deciding together? Skip the "I don't mind, you choose" loop:

  • Take turns planning date night — you'll both discover new places.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good restaurant for a first date?

Somewhere relaxed and mid-priced where you can hear each other, such as a tapas or small-plates bar, a wine bar with snacks, a good pizza place or a food market.

How do you choose a restaurant for an anniversary?

Pick something that feels special — a tasting menu, a chef's counter, a table with a view, or the place you had your first date — and mention the occasion when you book.

How can couples decide where to go for date night?

Take turns planning, each suggest three places and agree on one, or both swipe through nearby restaurants and go with the first match.
