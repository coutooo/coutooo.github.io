How to find a good restaurant in a new city

You're in a new city, it's 9pm and everyone's hungry. Here's how to skip the tourist traps and find somewhere good, quickly.

By Manuel Couto Updated 15 Nov 2026 6 min read
🧭 🍝 🗺️

How to spot a tourist trap

  • Someone outside inviting you in. Good places rarely need to.
  • A menu in six languages with photos of every dish.
  • Right on the main square or next to the big landmark.
  • Huge menu covering pizza, paella, curry and burgers.
  • Nobody local inside at a time locals would be eating.

Walk two or three streets away from the main sights and prices drop while quality goes up.

How to read reviews properly

  1. Look at the number of reviews, not just the stars. 4.6 from 800 reviews beats 5.0 from 12.
  2. Read the recent ones. Restaurants change chefs and owners.
  3. Check reviews in the local language. They tell you what locals think.
  4. Look at photos from guests, not the restaurant's own.
  5. Skim the 3-star reviews. They're the most honest about trade-offs.

Ask the right people

Skip "where should I eat?" and ask "where do you go for dinner?". Good people to ask: your host, a barista, a shop owner, the person at a market stall. Hotel concierges are helpful too, but some have arrangements with specific restaurants.

Frequently asked questions

How do you avoid tourist trap restaurants?

Walk a few streets away from the main sights, avoid places with staff calling you in and multilingual photo menus, and check for locals eating inside.

Are Google reviews reliable for restaurants?

They're a good guide when a place has many recent reviews. Read recent and mid-rated reviews and look at guest photos for a realistic picture.

How do I choose a restaurant quickly with friends?

Shortlist a few places that are open and in budget, then vote. Apps like Dinner let everyone swipe and show the place you all agree on.
