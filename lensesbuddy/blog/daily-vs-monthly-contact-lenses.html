Choosing lenses

Daily vs monthly contact lenses: which should you choose?

Both work brilliantly for millions of people — but they suit very different routines. Here's an honest comparison to help you have a better conversation with your optician.

By Manuel Couto Updated 26 Oct 2026 6 min read
📅 🆚 🗓️

The basic difference

Daily disposables are worn once and thrown away at night. There's no cleaning, no case and no solution. Monthly lenses are taken out each night, cleaned, stored in solution and reused for about 30 days from opening before you start a fresh pair.

Two-weekly lenses sit in between: same care routine as monthlies, replaced every 14 days.

Side-by-side comparison

Daily disposables Monthly lenses
Care routine None — new pair every day Clean and store every night
Hygiene Fresh, clean lens each morning Depends on good cleaning habits
Extra supplies None Solution and a case
Part-time wear Ideal — open one only when needed Pack keeps "ageing" once opened
Travel Very easy Need to pack solution and case
Packaging waste More (a blister per day) Less
Cost for full-time wear Usually higher per year Usually lower, plus solution
What to track Nothing — or just your supply Opening date and case age

Who daily lenses suit best

  • Part-time wearers — weekends, sport, nights out.
  • People with allergies or sensitive eyes — a fresh lens every day means no build-up of deposits (ask your optician if this applies to you).
  • Frequent travellers — nothing to pack but the lenses.
  • Anyone who knows they'll skip the cleaning routine. Be honest with yourself: the safest lens is the one you'll actually look after properly.

Who monthly lenses suit best

  • Full-time, everyday wearers who want to keep costs down.
  • People with a consistent routine who'll clean and store lenses properly every night.
  • Some prescriptions — certain toric (astigmatism) or specialist designs may be more widely available as reusable lenses. Your optician will know.
  • Anyone who wants less packaging waste.
💡

The catch with monthlies: they only work well if you replace them on time. Wearing a pair for six weeks because you lost track of the date is one of the most common lens mistakes.

Thinking about cost

Prices vary a lot by brand, prescription and where you buy, so compare like for like:

  • For dailies, multiply the price per lens by the number of days you actually wear them. If you only wear lenses two or three days a week, dailies can work out surprisingly reasonable.
  • For monthlies, add the cost of solution and a new case every three months to the price of the lenses.
  • Don't stretch lenses past their schedule to save money — the risk isn't worth it.

Can you switch between types?

Often, yes — and some people use both: monthlies for everyday, dailies for holidays or sport. But lenses differ in material, fit and curvature, so ask your optician before switching. You may need a fitting or a trial pair to make sure the new lenses suit your eyes.

Frequently asked questions

Are daily contact lenses healthier than monthly lenses?

Dailies remove the need for cleaning and storage, which takes away a common source of mistakes. Monthlies are also safe when they're cleaned properly and replaced on schedule. Your optician can advise which suits your eyes.

Are daily lenses more expensive than monthlies?

For full-time wear, dailies usually cost more per year. For part-time wear, they can be competitive because you only open a pair on the days you need it.

Can I wear daily lenses more than once?

No. Daily disposables are designed to be worn once and thrown away. They aren't made to be cleaned and stored.

Can I switch from monthly to daily lenses on my own?

Talk to your optician first. Different lenses have different materials and fits, and you may need a fitting or a trial pair.

This article is for general information only and does not replace advice from your eye care professional.
