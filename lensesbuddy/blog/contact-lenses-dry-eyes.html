Contact lenses and dry eyes: causes and 9 fixes that help

Gritty, tired, dry eyes by mid-afternoon are one of the most common reasons people give up on contact lenses. Often the cause is fixable.

By Manuel Couto Updated 15 Nov 2026 6 min read
💧 👁️ 🌬️

Why contact lenses can make your eyes feel dry

A contact lens sits in your tear film. It can soak up some of that moisture, and it can make tears evaporate faster. Add screens (we blink much less when we stare at them), air conditioning, heating or a lens worn past its replacement date, and dryness builds up through the day.

9 fixes that help

  1. Replace your lenses on time. Old lenses collect deposits and feel drier. See how often to replace them.
  2. Use rewetting drops made for contact lenses. Not all eye drops are lens-compatible; check the label.
  3. Blink on purpose at screens. Try the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look 20 feet (6 m) away for 20 seconds.
  4. Drink enough water. Dehydration shows up in your eyes too.
  5. Point air vents away from your face in the car and at your desk.
  6. Take breaks from lenses. Glasses in the evening or one day a week give your eyes a rest.
  7. Never sleep in lenses unless your eye doctor prescribed extended wear. Here's why.
  8. Keep your case clean. A dirty case can irritate your eyes. See how to clean it.
  9. Ask about a different lens or solution. Materials and solutions vary a lot; a switch can make a big difference.

When to see your eye doctor

🩺

Take your lenses out and contact your eye care professional if you have eye pain, redness that doesn't go away, blurred vision, sensitivity to light or discharge. These can be signs of an infection, not just dryness.

Persistent dryness is also worth a check-up. Your optometrist can look at your tear film and recommend lenses, drops or treatment that suit you.

Frequently asked questions

Why do my contact lenses feel dry at the end of the day?

Lenses can absorb moisture and speed up tear evaporation, and screens make you blink less. Old lenses, air conditioning and long wear times make it worse.

Can I use any eye drops with contact lenses?

No. Use drops labelled as suitable for contact lenses, or ask your pharmacist or eye doctor. Some drops should only be used with lenses out.

Should I stop wearing contacts if my eyes are dry?

Take them out if your eyes are painful, red or your vision is blurred, and see your eye doctor. Mild dryness can often be fixed with a different lens, solution or routine.
