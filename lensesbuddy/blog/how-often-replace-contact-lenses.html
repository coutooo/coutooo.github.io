Eye care guide

How often should you replace contact lenses?

Daily, two-weekly or monthly — the rule is simpler than it looks. Here's exactly when to swap each type, the lens-case rule most people forget, and an easy way to never lose count.

By Manuel Couto Updated 26 Oct 2026 7 min read
👁️ 📅 🔔
ℹ️

This guide is general information, not medical advice. Your optician or eye doctor knows your eyes and your lenses — always follow the schedule they give you.

The short answer

Replace your lenses according to their replacement schedule — the one printed on the box and confirmed by your eye care professional. In practice, that means:

Lens type Replace after Good to know
Daily disposable Every day Wear once, bin at night. Never store and reuse.
Two-weekly 14 days Clean and store in fresh solution every night.
Monthly ~30 days The most common type — and the easiest to lose count of.
Quarterly / long-life As prescribed Some lenses last 3 months or more; follow your optician exactly.

The tricky part isn't knowing the number. It's remembering which day you opened the pair — especially when you wear lenses on some days and glasses on others.

When does the clock start?

For most two-weekly and monthly lenses, the countdown starts the day you open the blister pack, not the day you first wear them and not "after 30 wears".

Why? Once a lens is out of its sealed packaging it starts collecting protein and lipid deposits from your tears and from handling, even while it sits in solution. Over time that build-up makes lenses less comfortable, can blur your vision, and gives bacteria something to cling to.

💡

Part-time wearer? If you only wear lenses at weekends or for sport, ask your optician whether daily disposables make more sense. You open a fresh pair only on the days you need them, and there's nothing to keep track of.

Signs a lens needs replacing early

A schedule is a maximum, not a target. Swap your lenses sooner if you notice:

  • Discomfort that wasn't there when the pair was new.
  • Blurry or hazy vision that doesn't clear after cleaning.
  • A tear, nick or folded edge — damaged lenses can scratch your eye.
  • Visible deposits or a cloudy look.
  • Dropping a lens somewhere you can't be sure is clean.

If your eye is red, painful, very sensitive to light, or your vision changes suddenly, take your lenses out and contact an eye care professional straight away rather than just putting in a new pair.

Don't forget the case (and the solution)

Your lens case is where your lenses spend every night, so it matters just as much. The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) recommends replacing your contact lens case at least once every three months.

A few more habits that keep your lenses — and your eyes — happy:

  • Use fresh solution every time. Don't "top up" yesterday's solution in the case.
  • Rub and rinse your lenses with solution as the instructions say, even with "no-rub" products if your optician recommends it.
  • Empty the case, rinse it with solution (not water) and leave it open to air-dry upside down.
  • Keep lenses away from water — that includes tap water, showers and swimming.
  • Wash and dry your hands before touching your lenses.

Can you sleep in contact lenses?

Generally, no. Sleeping in lenses reduces the oxygen reaching your cornea and is linked to a higher risk of eye infections. The CDC's advice is to avoid sleeping in lenses unless your eye care provider has told you it's OK — some lenses are approved for extended wear, but that's a decision for your optician, not the box alone.

Fell asleep in them by accident? Take them out as soon as you wake up, give your eyes a break with glasses, and get checked if your eyes stay red or sore.

How to actually remember your swap date

Most people who over-wear lenses aren't careless — they simply forget when they opened the pair. Some tricks that work:

  1. Start on the 1st. If you wear monthlies, open a new pair on the first of each month so the date is easy to remember.
  2. Write it on the box. A marker on the packaging works — until you lose the box.
  3. Set a phone reminder. Better, but one-off alarms are easy to dismiss and forget to reset.
  4. Use a lens tracker. An app that counts down for you, shows the days left on your home screen and reminds you on the right day.

Frequently asked questions

Can I wear monthly lenses for longer than a month?

No. Monthly lenses are designed to be replaced after about 30 days from opening. Past that point deposits build up and the material can let less oxygen through, which raises the risk of irritation and infection.

If I only wear monthlies a few days a week, do they last longer?

Usually not. Most manufacturers count from the day you open the pack, not the number of days worn, because the lens keeps ageing in its solution. Check your lens box and ask your optician about your specific brand.

Can I reuse daily disposable lenses?

No. Daily disposables are made to be worn once and thrown away. They aren't designed to be cleaned and stored overnight.

How often should I replace my lens case?

At least once every three months, according to the CDC — and sooner if it's cracked or dirty.

How many hours a day can I wear contact lenses?

It depends on your lenses and your eyes, so ask your optician. If your eyes feel dry, gritty or tired, that's a sign to take them out and switch to glasses for the rest of the day.

Sources: CDC — Preventing Eye Infections When Wearing Contacts. This article is for general information only and does not replace advice from your eye care professional.
