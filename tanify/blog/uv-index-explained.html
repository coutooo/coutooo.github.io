UV index

UV index explained: what each number means for your skin

The weather app says "UV 7". Is that a lot? The UV index is the single most useful number for planning time in the sun, and it's simpler than it looks.

By Manuel Couto Updated 15 Nov 2026 6 min read
☀️ 📈 🧴

What the UV index measures

The UV index is a scale, used worldwide, that tells you how strong the sun's ultraviolet radiation is at ground level. It doesn't measure heat. A cool, breezy spring day at the beach or in the mountains can have a higher UV index than a hot, hazy summer afternoon.

The higher the number, the faster UV can damage your skin and eyes.

The scale, level by level

UV indexLevelWhat to do
0–2LowMinimal protection needed for most people. Sunglasses on bright days.
3–5ModerateSunscreen, hat and sunglasses. Seek shade around midday.
6–7HighProtection is essential. Reduce time in the sun between late morning and mid-afternoon.
8–10Very highExtra precautions. Unprotected skin can burn quickly.
11+ExtremeAvoid the midday sun. Cover up, use shade and SPF 30+.

Health agencies generally recommend sun protection whenever the UV index is 3 or higher.

What makes the UV index go up

  • Time of day: UV usually peaks around solar noon, roughly 11:00 to 15:00 depending on where you are.
  • Season and latitude: higher in summer and closer to the equator.
  • Altitude: UV increases as you go higher, which is why people burn fast when skiing or hiking.
  • Reflection: snow, sand, water and concrete bounce UV back at you.
  • Clouds: thin cloud blocks much less UV than you'd think. You can still burn on an overcast day.

How fast can you burn?

It depends on your skin type and the UV index. Very fair skin can start to burn in well under 15 minutes at a high UV index, while darker skin takes longer, but every skin type can be damaged by UV. There is no completely safe tan: a tan is your skin's response to UV damage.

⚠️

Rule of thumb: if your shadow is shorter than you are, the sun is strong. That's the time for shade, a hat and sunscreen.

Frequently asked questions

What UV index is safe?

A UV index of 0 to 2 is low risk for most people. From 3 upward, health agencies recommend sunscreen, a hat, sunglasses and shade around midday.

Can you burn when it's cloudy?

Yes. Thin or broken cloud lets most UV through, so you can still burn on a cloudy day, especially when the UV index is moderate or higher.

What time of day is the UV index highest?

Usually around solar noon, roughly between 11:00 and 15:00. Check an hourly UV forecast for your location to see the exact peak.
